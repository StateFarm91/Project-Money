# Integrator reading of the owner's visual direction (2026-11-02)

This is a reading by eye of images that do not come from a measured source. It is a review rubric and a
gap finder, not a certification. Geometry, identity and scale must be judged by the pipeline's own gates
against the certified CIR and the canonical pack, and those gates decide.

## What the examples get right (the standard to reproduce)
- **One coherent home across frames 01-04 and 09.**
  - The same cream armchair with a sage throw, oak dresser with round wooden knobs, eucalyptus in a
    stone vase, framed botanical prints, linen curtains and window light, and a wooden pull-toy.
  - Room geometry stays consistent (window left, dresser right). It reads as one real Canadian
    family home, not a showroom.
- **The product is consistent across frames.**
  - Six-sided body, cream wall with two burgundy one-round stripes, braided rim; frame 05 also shows a
    braided base edge.
  - Vertical "knit-look" columns, read as waistcoat-style single crochet.
- **The gallery has a full range of roles:** Laura holding the product for scale, a close-up of the stitch and
  rim, a wide styled shot, a use shot (filled), and a clean pack shot (05).
- **Natural light, shallow depth, believable clutter.**

## Defects and truth risks the automatic pipeline must catch
1. **Construction must match the CIR, not the picture.** Treat the stitch texture (waistcoat-style), the
   braided rim and base edge, the hexagonal shape and the stripe placement as claims. If the certified
   design is plain single crochet, has no braided edge, or places its stripes differently, then
   every one of these images is a Product Truth failure, however good it looks.
2. **The shape is ambiguous at some angles.** In 02 and 04 the hexagon reads close to a rounded rectangle.
   The gallery needs at least one frame where the side count is unambiguous (05 and 03 do this), and the
   geometry review must not accept the ambiguous frames as evidence.
3. **Name and staging do not match.** A "Bread Basket" is staged in a nursery and filled with baby muslins,
   booties and a wooden ring that resembles a teether (04).
   - This is a name/use claim mismatch, in the A2 class.
   - It places the product in a children's context, in the A1-R class, although a storage basket is not a
     children's product.
   - "Bread" also implies food contact, which needs its own claim check.
   - The scene must follow the certified product name and use.
4. **Near-duplicates.** 03 and 09 have the same composition, so a gallery should not carry both.
   Gallery assembly needs a de-duplication rule.
5. **Scale is plausible but unmeasured.** The product reads as about 30 cm across against Laura's hands
   and the dresser. Each frame needs its scale checked against the CIR's finished dimensions, with
   known-size scene objects (the dresser, the armchair) as anchors.
6. **Laura (01).** She resembles the canonical identity, but hair tone and styling differ from the
   reference pack. Only the identity gate against laura-r2-a42aeac7 decides. A lifestyle frame is never a new
   reference.
7. **Disclosure.** All six basket frames are generated (generator unattested). None of them is
   physical-sample photography, and none may be presented as such.

## Competitor benchmark (10): intelligence only
The competitor gallery uses this sequence: a model holding a nested set, the size set, styled with
a plant, a texture close-up, and a grouped set. Its reviews raise a recurring concern that the basket is
"a bit flimsy" with yarn as the base. That is a demand signal: Brambleloop galleries and copy should show and state
structure (strand, density, base). Do not copy its composition or photography.
