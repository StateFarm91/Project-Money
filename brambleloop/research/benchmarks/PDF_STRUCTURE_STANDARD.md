# Premium pattern-PDF benchmark — STRUCTURE ONLY (owner-supplied competitor PDF, 2026-11-19)

> Family-aware (owner, 2026-11-19): garments need the full structure; small home goods need a concise but complete PDF (materials, gauge, finished size, abbreviations, every row/round with counts, finishing, licence, disclosure).
Use: merchandising/format intelligence. NEVER copy text, stitch definitions, numbers, schematics, photos, or layout art.
Our content must come only from our certified CIR / compiler output.

Benchmark section order (what a premium buyer expects):
1. Cover: product name, designer brand, a full-bleed hero photo, skill level, website footer.
2. Project overview: a short description, construction summary, size range, and a details table (skill level with icon, sizes, fit, yarn weight with CYC number, recommended yarn, hook sizes, construction method, tutorial link/QR) beside a lifestyle photo.
3. Supply list: yarn (fibre, yd/g per ball, colours shown), hooks (US letter + mm, "or size to obtain gauge"), notions (buttons with mm size, markers, needle), and GAUGE for EACH fabric (main stitch and ribbing, over 4"/2", with the hook).
4. Construction narrative: a plain-language paragraph on piece order, plus a progress photo of the flat piece.
5. Size chart table, with chest/body measurement vs FINISHED measurement, plus yarn quantity per size (balls AND yards). Fit/ease note ("N–M in positive ease"), and a yardage disclaimer suggesting an extra ball.
6. Schematic: every key measurement per size (back/front width, underarm-to-hem, sleeve width/length, neck, hood), with band/cuff add-ons stated separately.
7. Project tips: 5–8 practical bullets (markers, tension at joins, count checks, read-through-first).
8. Abbreviations, labelled "US terms", and a Special stitches section with full written definitions.
9. Pattern: a two-column layout; the smallest size first with others in parentheses throughout; bold row labels with RS/WS; a stitch count after EVERY row; section headers per piece; inline mini-diagrams at the tricky steps (sleeve join, neck marking); size-specific branches clearly called out.
10. Every page: a running header with the product name, a footer with page number and copyright, and a consistent brand colour band.

Gaps vs our pipeline to check (ENG-VISUAL / PDF):
- A per-size yardage table (balls + yards), derived from compiler yarn estimates, not guessed.
- Gauge for every distinct fabric in the CIR.
- Finished vs body measurement plus a stated ease.
- A schematic page generated from CIR geometry (deterministic SVG), not image-model art.
- A stitch count after every row (the compiler already has these), and a size-parenthesis convention for multi-size patterns.
- Inline construction mini-diagrams generated from CIR.
- US/UK term variants (we ship two PDFs; the benchmark ships US only, which is an advantage for us).
- Tutorial video/QR: we have none; DO NOT claim one. Optional future.
- Our edge: certified stitch-count math, US+UK editions, honest product truth (the pictured item IS what the pattern makes).

## Amigurumi / stuffed-animal family addendum (2026-11-20, from TEARDOWN_HIGHLAND_COW_AMIGURUMI.md)

- **Pattern pages:** organised by component (head, body, limbs ×N, ears, tail, features,
  surface effects such as fur or hair). Each component has a header, a working note (rounds,
  joining, colour), round lines with a stitch count on EVERY round, and inline "stuff now"
  cues.
- **Per-component image slot:** a real progress photo when a physical sample exists;
  otherwise a CIR-generated component diagram labelled as a diagram. Never a fake photo.
- **Assembly:** numbered steps, each with a placement cue tied to round numbers and an image
  slot.
- **Mandatory:** finished size (from CIR), gauge, grams and yardage per colour, and safety
  and age guidance from the child-safety gate (embroidered-feature option, strand anchoring
  for fur or hair).

  delivery.
