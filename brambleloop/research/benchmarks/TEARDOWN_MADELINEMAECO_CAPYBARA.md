## 1. Structure (10 pages)

1. **Cover:** the character name in bold sans, "a pattern by: <shop>", and ONE real photo of
   the finished toy held up against a brick wall (the same photo as the listing hero). A
   copyright footer on every page.
2. **Notes + abbreviations + materials (one page, two columns):**
   - terms (US), continuous rounds, the marker tip, and the designer's handedness and yarn
     hold;
   - 9 abbreviations, including the count-notation convention;
   - materials: hook in mm, a NAMED commercial chenille yarn with named colourways, black
     cotton for the face, notions, stuffing, and safety eye size.
3. **Pattern pages, by component, smallest first:** arms → legs → ears → body → head →
   accessory. Each piece has its colour, "make two", round lines with counts, and inline
   cues ("lightly stuff", "don't stuff anymore going forward", "fold like a sandwich").
   **Real progress photos sit beside nearly every component** (pieces held in hand).
4. **Join-as-you-go body:** limbs are crocheted INTO specific body rounds, with notes that
   placement may need adjusting for the spiral. An annotated photo marks the stitch path, and
   the head is crocheted onto the body. This is what earns "no sew / low sew" in the title.
5. **Eye options, a whole page of photo comparisons:** embroidered eyes / safety eyes before
   shaping / after shaping. **A safer, embroidered option is offered and shown.**
6. **Face-shaping technique page:** a numbered 5-step method with photos and red arrows
   (photos reused from another of their patterns, which the page says).
7. **Finishing:** ear placement relative to rounds and eyes, shown with photo arrows.
8. **Accessory + finishing:** a small seasonal topper (fruit + leaf), an embroidered nose,
   and three photos of the finished toy.
9. **About the designer:** a real photo of the designer, a short bio, and shop and social
   links.

## 2. Why it wins (what we must match or beat)

- **M1. A product-market fit that is ultra-specific:**
  - chunky chenille (fast, soft, forgiving, plush look);
  - small piece count;
  - **low-sew / no-sew construction** (the #1 pain point of amigurumi makers is sewing
    pieces on), named in the title;
  - a seasonal or cute accessory as a variant hook;
  - a named, lovable character.
- **M2. Real photos at every step.** The single strongest trust driver. Reviews say "easy to
  follow" and "allowed me to learn a new skill".
- **M3. Plain, fast-to-read layout.** No decoration competes with the instructions, so
  premium design is NOT what sells here. Clarity, photos and the product are.
- **M4. A choice of eyes with a visual comparison** (embroidered vs safety). It is
  child-friendlier and widens the audience.
- **M5. Placement cues tied to round numbers and stitch counts between features**, plus
  arrows on photos.
- **M6. Merchandising:**
  - one hero photo identical to the cover, held in hand for scale against a textured real
    wall;
  - 7+ gallery images including colour and season variants;
  - a price in the low impulse band.

## 3. Defects (classes only; our edge)

| # | Defect class | Observed | Brambleloop answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | No gauge, no finished size | Neither is stated. A review reports stitches "too tight" with the stated hook and suggests a larger one. **Gauge ambiguity is a real customer complaint.** | Gauge and finished size from the CIR. For chenille or blanket yarn, a "fabric should feel…" note plus a tension check swatch, and a hook-size range. |
| D2 | No yardage or quantities | Colours only; no amounts. | Grams and yardage per colour from the twin. |
| D3 | Ambiguous count notation | Multi-round lines give a cumulative total in brackets, which reads as a per-round count. | One convention: the count per round, always. Repeats are stated as "Rounds a–b: … (n)". |
| D4 | US only | | US and UK editions. |
| D5 | Safety | The embroidered-eye option is good, but there is no age guidance or small-parts note for safety eyes, and no chenille shedding or care note. | The child-safety gate: age guidance, embroidered-feature default for baby or under-3, care and inspection notes. |
| D6 | Reused photos from another pattern | Disclosed on the page, but the item pictured differs. | Product Truth: images show the item the pattern makes. |

## 5. Lessons for each department (ENG-LEARN / ENG-IMPROVE ingest)

- **L1, Concepts and research (CONCEPTS-100, research flow).** Prioritise **chunky chenille,
  low-sew amigurumi animals with a lovable character name and an optional seasonal
  accessory**. Small piece count; quick make (an evening). Price band: effective about
  CA$5, anchor about CA$9. Ideas must be ORIGINAL animals, characters and constructions:
  never a capybara with a fruit, never "Cara".
- **L2, Engineering (PLUSH / amigurumi engine).** Build and verify a **join-as-you-go
  ("low-sew") construction capability** in the CIR:
  - limbs and appendages worked closed flat ("fold and close"), then crocheted into a named
    body round at stated stitch positions;
  - the head joined by working through both pieces.

  Compiler, twin and reverse must validate the joined stitch counts. This is a technique
  class, not a protected design. Our implementation, wording and geometry are our own.
- **L3, Yarn model.** Add **chenille / blanket yarn (CYC 6–7 jumbo)** as a first-class yarn
  class: gauge, a hook-size range, tension guidance ("work tighter than you think"), and a
  shedding and care note.
- **L4, PDF (TEMPLATE / PREMIUM / SALE-PIPELINE).**
  - Progress imagery at EVERY component and assembly step is the strongest trust driver.
  - Without a physical sample, use CIR-generated component and assembly diagrams labelled as
    diagrams, with arrow callouts for placement. Never present a render as a progress photo.
  - Offer an **eye options** panel (embroidered vs safety) with age guidance.
  - Keep the pattern pages clean: decoration must not compete with instructions (our
    template already does this).
- **L5, Visual / listing.** The hero is the finished toy held in hand for scale against a
  real textured background, identical to the PDF cover. Variant images (colourways, seasonal
  accessory) fill the gallery. That matches our 7-photo campaign roles: hero, scale/fit,
  variants.
- **L6, SEO.**
  - Title: `<Character name> the <animal> | amigurumi <animal> pattern, no sew, low sew, …`.
  - "no sew" and "low sew" are high-intent modifiers. Use them ONLY when our certified
    construction truly is low-sew.
  - The disclaimer "THIS IS NOT A FINISHED PRODUCT" goes at the top of the description.
- **L7, Reviews to learn from.** "Stitches too tight with the stated hook" is a pattern
  defect our gauge and hook-range section prevents. The learning loop records it as a lesson
  class: *missing gauge → tension complaints*.
