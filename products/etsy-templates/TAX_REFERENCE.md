## GST/HST quick method (RC4058)
Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/rc4058/quick-method-accounting-gst-hst.html

Eligibility: annual worldwide taxable supplies (GST/HST included) ≤ $400,000 for the first four consecutive fiscal quarters of the last five. Election via My Business Account or Form GST74; stays in effect at least one year. 1% credit on the first $30,000 of eligible supplies each fiscal year.

## Motor vehicle (self-employed)
Self-employed individuals deduct ACTUAL vehicle costs (fuel, insurance, maintenance, licence, lease or loan interest, CCA) multiplied by business km ÷ total km; a cents-per-km claim is not acceptable on T2125. The 2026 CRA allowance rates (73¢ first 5,000 km, 67¢ after; 77¢/71¢ in the territories) apply to employee allowances, not to T2125 claims; the template shows them only as a reasonableness reference. Sources: https://www.driversnote.ca/cra-mileage-guide , https://sparkreceipt.com/en-ca/blog/cra-mileage-rate-2026/ , https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/benefits-allowances/automobile/automobile-motor-vehicle-allowances.html

## Business-use-of-home
Conditions: the home is the principal place of business (>50% of work), OR the space is used exclusively to earn business income and on a regular and continuous basis to meet clients/customers/patients. Eligible costs: heat, electricity, insurance, maintenance, mortgage interest, property taxes, rent, other; prorated by area (and by time if shared space). The claim cannot create or increase a loss; the excess carries forward. Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/completing-form-t2125/calculating-business-use-home-expenses.html 

T2125 Part 7 lines (business-use-of-home): 7A heat, 7B electricity, 7C insurance, 7D maintenance, 7E mortgage interest, 7F property taxes, 7G other expenses, 7K CCA (business portion only); personal-use part subtracted by area proportion (example on the page: personal 162 ÷ 180 of $13,300); 7L carry-forward from previous year; 7M total available; 7N net income after adjustments (cap); 7O amount to carry forward = 7M − 7N (not below 0); 7P allowable claim = lesser of 7M and 7N, which flows to line 9945.

## 2026 CPP (self-employed), for the instalment planner
Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/payroll-deductions-contributions/canada-pension-plan-cpp/cpp-contribution-rates-maximums-exemptions.html , cross-checked against https://www.advisor.ca/tax/tax-news/cra-announces-cpp-maximum-pensionable-earnings-for-2026/
- Basic exemption: $3,500 (unchanged).
- Base CPP: self-employed rate 11.9% on earnings between $3,500 and the year's maximum pensionable earnings (YMPE) $74,600. Maximum base contribution: $8,460.90.
- CPP2 (enhanced, second tier): self-employed rate 8% on earnings between $74,600 and the additional maximum pensionable earnings (YAMPE) $85,000. Maximum CPP2 contribution: $832.00.
- Combined self-employed maximum: $9,292.90.
- Quebec residents contribute to QPP, not CPP; QPP rates are close to but not identical to CPP. The planner uses the CPP rate as an approximation for Quebec and discloses this.

## CRA instalment rule of thumb (unchanged; already in the plan)
Instalments required if net tax owing exceeds $3,000 in the current year AND in either of the two prior years (Quebec: $1,800, because Quebec residents also pay provincial instalments to Revenu Québec). Due dates: March 15, June 15, September 15, December 15. Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/making-payments-cra/if-you-a-required-to-make-instalment-payments.html
